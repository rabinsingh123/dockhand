# Impact facts for Dockhand (cited)

Read 2026-10-27. Every quote below was read on the source page or PDF unless it is marked **Unverified**. Portuguese quotes carry our translation in brackets. "Our read" marks our interpretation; everything else is what the source says.

Strength key:
- **Primary**: published by whoever measured it.
- **Commissioned**: primary, but paid for by a party with an interest (Meta, a vendor).
- **Vendor survey**: run by a company that sells a fix for the problem.
- **Secondary**: press or blog reporting someone else's number.
- **Unverified**: seen only in search-engine snippets; the page blocked us or timed out. Open it in a browser before use.

Several sources block scripted fetchers (403): coupa.com, sap.com product pages, businesswire.com, gartner.com, channelfutures.com, apqc.org. Coupa's page loaded in a browser; check the others the same way before relying on them.

## 2. Sales rep time on non-selling work and data entry

**2.1 Where the week goes.** Primary.
Salesforce, State of Sales, 7th edition (2026). "Salesforce surveyed 4,050 sales professionals" in 22 countries; "anonymous survey conducted from August through September, 2025". The sample includes Brazil (N=250), India (N=250), Mexico (N=200) and Singapore (N=100), not Indonesia (p.36).

Chart "How Reps Spend Their Time During an Average Workweek" (p.8), labels as printed: **40% Selling, 60% Not selling.** Segments: Meeting with customers 22%, Prospecting 18%, **Creating quotes 17%**, Planning 16%, **Manually entering data 13%**, Training 11%, Other 3%.
> "They spend more than half of their time on nonselling work like data entry and prospecting." (p.8)

Our read: the 40% "Selling" wedge lines up with the meeting (22%) and prospecting (18%) segments, while the body text calls prospecting nonselling. Quote the labels as printed and do not re-sum segments on screen.
https://www.salesforce.com/en-us/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/documents/reports/sales/salesforce-state-of-sales-report-2026.pdf

**2.2 Same finding, Salesforce web page.** Primary.
> "Sales reps spend 60% of their time on non-selling tasks."

Salesforce, sales statistics page, 3 Feb 2026, citing the 7th edition.
https://www.salesforce.com/sales/state-of-sales/sales-statistics/

**2.3 Tool sprawl.** Primary (same report, p.16).
> "Only a third of sales teams use an all-in-one platform. The rest use a mix of standalone tools — an average of eight per team — risking tech bloat. Nearly half of reps say they're overwhelmed by the volume."
> "42% of sales reps are overwhelmed by too many tools."

**2.4 After-hours admin and tool switching.** Vendor survey, US only.
> "Overtime has become the norm across the industry, with 85% of professionals working late at least once a week as manual administrative tasks spill beyond standard hours."
> "Nearly four in five professionals (79%) must switch between multiple tools to get a complete view of a customer account."
> "Only 11% of sales professionals count closing deals among their most frequent weekly activities" ... "compared with 38% who spend most of their time logging calls, emails, and meetings"

Pipedrive, CRM Trends Report 2026, press release 4 Aug 2026. Online survey of 1,000 U.S. sales and marketing professionals, "conducted in June 2026".
https://www.pipedrive.com/en/newsroom/the-modern-salesperson-is-becoming-a-data-entry-clerk

**2.5 Adoption signals.** Primary (same Salesforce report, p.10 and p.2).
> "Nine in 10 sales teams use agents today or expect to within two years."
> "Key use cases include: fulfilling orders to improve customer experience, tracking product usage to deliver usage pricing, and creating quotes to speed up the closing process."
> "And 94% of sales leaders with agents say they're essential to growth."

Not found: a credible stat specifically on reps re-entering the same data into several systems. 2.3 and 2.4 (tool switching) are the closest.

## 5. Brittleness of UI automation (RPA)

**5.1 Breakage frequency.** Vendor-commissioned. **Unverified**: Business Wire blocked us; the sentence is identical across several search-indexed copies. Confirm in a browser before use.
> "Forty-five percent of firms deal with bot breakage on a weekly basis or more often, and in the meantime, customer experience is impacted."

Forrester Consulting study commissioned by Tricentis, release dated 12 Feb 2020; enterprise decision makers with RPA programs.
https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20200212005226/en/RPA-Reality-Check-New-Forrester-Research-Identifies-Barriers-to-RPA-Scalability

**5.2 Failures and upkeep.** Vendor survey.
> "Already, 87% of respondents experience some level of bot failures."
> "Forty-one percent of respondents said that ongoing bot management is taking more time and resources than expected."

Pegasystems, 10 Sep 2019. 509 decision makers at businesses using RPA or RDA, minimum $100 million revenue.
https://www.pega.com/node/80501

**5.3 Weekly broken bots.** Vendor survey, secondary report, sample size not given.
> "69% of respondents experience broken bots with their current RPA at least once per week"

Robocorp "State of RPA 2022" (respondents: RPA users in IT teams), reported by VentureBeat, 30 Jun 2022.
https://venturebeat.com/2022/06/30/report-81-of-it-teams-plan-to-invest-in-rpa-next-year

**5.4 Project failure, not UI breakage.** Primary (EY field experience, not a survey).
> "we have seen as many as 30 to 50% of initial RPA projects fail. This isn't a reflection of the technology; there are many successful deployments."

EY, "Get ready for robots" (Chris Lamberton), ©2016.
https://www.eyfinancialservicesthoughtgallery.ie/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/ey-get-ready-for-robots.pdf

**5.5 Scaling.** Primary. Not about UI changes.
> "Thirteen per cent of our survey respondents stated they are now implementing automation at scale."
> "Process fragmentation and a lack of IT readiness remain the top two barriers, as they have in the past two years."

Deloitte, "Automation with intelligence: Pursuing organisation-wide reimagination". Survey in mid-2020 of 441 executives from 29 countries; "at scale" means 51+ automations.
https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/insights/articles/2024/73699-global-intelligent-automation-survey/DI_Automation-with-intelligence.pdf

Not found: a Gartner statistic on bots breaking from UI changes; any primary source for RPA maintenance as a share of program cost.

Counterpoint judges may raise (headline only; gartner.com blocked us): Gartner, 25 Jun 2025, "Gartner Predicts Over 40% of Agentic AI Projects Will Be Canceled by End of 2027".

## 6. Computer-use agents: cost and latency vs scripted automation

**6.1 Latency.** Primary (academic).
> "However, these systems are practically unusable due to extremely high end-to-end latency (e.g., tens of minutes) for tasks that typically take humans just a few minutes to complete."
> "We find that large model calls for planning and reflection account for the majority of the overall latency, and as an agent uses more steps to complete a task, each successive step can take 3x longer than steps at the beginning of a task."
> "even the highest-scoring agents on OSWorld take 1.4-2.7x more steps than necessary."

Abhyankar, Qi, Zhang, "OSWorld-Human: Benchmarking the Efficiency of Computer-Use Agents", arXiv 2506.16042 v1, 19 Jun 2025. v2 (18 May 2026) changes the step figure to "2.7-4.3x more steps than necessary"; cite the version you show.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16042v1 (latest: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16042)

Not found: a credible public benchmark of cost per task for computer-use agents vs scripted replay. Figures in circulation are vendor blog estimates. Our own eval (first agentic run vs compiled replay, 50 runs per skill, promise 5 in `docs/SUBMISSION.md`) is the evidence to show.

Counterpoint judges may raise (headline only; gartner.com blocked us): Gartner, 18 Nov 2025, "Gartner Predicts By 2028 AI Agents Will Outnumber Sellers by 10X—Yet Fewer Than 40% of Sellers Will Report AI Agents Improved Productivity".
https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-11-18-gartner-predicts-by-2028-ai-agents-will-outnumber-sellers-by-10x-yet-fewer-than-40-percent-of-sellers-will-report-ai-agents-improved-productivity
